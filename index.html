PIPELINE CORROSION MONTE CARLO

Probability of Through-Wall Leak
PoF in this application means probability of modeled through-wall corrosion penetration / pinhole leakage. It is not burst probability, rupture probability, pressure-capacity failure, or total pipeline failure probability from all threats. The model addresses corrosion through-wall penetration only. All calculations run locally in this browser; no data is transmitted.

Distribution fit check - Method B (Normal / Lognormal / Gamma)
Each distribution is fitted to the observed rates (Normal: mean/SD, truncated at 0 as sampled; Lognormal: mean/SD of ln CR, zeros excluded; Gamma: method of moments). Q-Q R² = squared correlation between observed and fitted quantiles (Blom positions) - closer to 1 means the points follow the fitted curve. KS D = largest gap between the observed and fitted cumulative curves (lower is better; the 5% reference value assumes known parameters, so treat it as a guide). AD A² = Anderson-Darling statistic, weighted toward the tails (lower is better). The upper tail (fast rates) drives early-year PoF, so check the top-right of the Q-Q plot.

Back-test (model validation)

For every pipeline and mechanism with two or more detections: hide the latest reading, predict it from the earlier data (fleet prior + earlier readings + sizing error of the hidden run), and check where the actual reading falls. A calibrated model puts about 80% of actual readings inside its 80% interval and about 90% inside its 90% interval, with no systematic bias. With only two inspections the prediction rests on the fleet prior, so the test checks how well the fleet data describe these pipelines.

Next ILI projection

Recommended next ILI = the earlier (more conservative) of:
  • Fixed interval: Last ILI date + fixed reassessment interval for the line's process fluid (editable table below).
  • Half remaining life: Last ILI date + 0.5 x remaining life, where remaining life is the Worst-Case time to through-wall penetration on the basis selected below (from the last Monte Carlo run).
Time zero for remaining life is the Last ILI date, because the remaining wall in the inventory comes from that inspection. Status is evaluated at the assessment date. A planned date later than the recommended date is flagged.

Data Interpretation - P10 / P50 / P90 estimates

Pxx is a percentile: the value that xx% of outcomes fall at or below. The tool uses percentiles in two places, and the direction of "bad" flips between them.

1. Corrosion-rate percentiles (CR Data tab)

Taken from the observed corrosion-rate dataset for each mechanism.

  • P10 CR - 10% of observed rates are at or below this value (slow end).
  • P50 CR - the median observed rate.
  • P90 CR - 90% of observed rates are at or below this value; 10% are faster (aggressive end).

For corrosion rate, a high percentile is the bad case.

2. Time-to-through-wall percentiles (Fleet Results, PoF Curves, Pipeline Details)

Taken from the N simulated TTF values for one pipeline and one mechanism.

  • P10 TTF - 10% of realizations penetrate at or before this time (early, fast-corrosion tail).
  • P50 TTF - the median penetration time; half penetrate earlier, half later.
  • P90 TTF - 90% of realizations have penetrated by this time (late end).

For TTF, a low percentile is the bad case. Because TTF = RW / CR, a fast rate gives a short life, so P10 TTF corresponds roughly to RW / P90 CR.

P10, P50 and P90 fall exactly on step edges, so sampling noise can land them on either neighbouring value (coloured line = lower value, grey line = upper value). This run reported 2.57 / 5.00 / 15.0 yr.

Link to the PoF curve

The cumulative PoF curve is the cumulative distribution of TTF. Pxx TTF is the time at which the curve reaches xx%: P10 TTF is where cumulative PoF crosses 10%, P50 TTF where it crosses 50%. Tick Show P10 / P50 / P90 markers on the Pipeline Details chart to see the read-off points, and use the TTF range chart on the PoF Curves tab to compare P10-P50-P90 across the fleet.

Worst-Case Pxx TTF = MIN(Internal Pxx TTF, External Pxx TTF). Because Worst-Case PoF is the higher of the two curves at every year, it reaches each percentile at the earlier of the two times. Combined (paired) has its own TTF distribution, MIN(TTF_int, TTF_ext) per realization; Combined under the independence assumption has no simulated TTF, so no Pxx is shown for it.

Cautions

  • Small datasets give jumpy percentiles. With 10 observations the TTF distribution has 10 steps of 10%. P10/P50/P90 sit on step edges, so a different seed or N can move them to the neighbouring observed value (P10 2.57 or 2.73; P50 5.00 or 6.00; P90 12.0 or 15.0 in the example). They settle as observations are added.
  • Oil and gas reserves use the opposite convention. In reserves estimation (e.g. SPE-PRMS), P90 means a 90% chance of exceeding the value, so P90 is the low case. This tool uses the statistical convention (at or below). Check which one a report uses before comparing numbers.
  • Infinite and beyond-horizon values. If more than xx% of draws use CR = 0, Pxx TTF is infinite (shown as ∞). Pxx TTF beyond the analysis horizon is valid but is not reached on the PoF curves, which stop at the horizon.
  • Pxx describes the model, not the pipe. All percentiles are conditional on the uploaded CR data being representative of the line, and on the remaining wall (and ILI uncertainty settings) used.

Practical use

  • P10 TTF - the conservative figure for setting re-inspection or mitigation timing.
  • P50 TTF - the typical expectation.
  • P10 to P90 spread - how uncertain the prediction is. A wide spread usually means the CR data is highly variable or sparse.

Distribution fit check and Method A vs Method B

  • Q-Q R² close to 1 means the observed rates follow the fitted distribution. Look hardest at the upper-right points: fast rates drive early-year PoF, and a fit that bends away there under- or over-states the early risk.
  • KS D and AD A²: lower is better. AD gives more weight to the tails. With small datasets several distributions can fit almost equally well - the choice then matters most in the tail.
  • Method A (bootstrap) cannot produce a rate faster than the fastest observation. Method B can, so it is usually more conservative early on; with a light-tailed fit (e.g. truncated Normal) it can also be less conservative. The A vs B tab shows which applies to each pipeline and year.
  • Differences within about 2 standard errors of the Monte Carlo estimate are noise, not a real difference between the methods; increase N to resolve small differences.

ILI measurement uncertainty - what "± represents" means

The tolerance is the half-width of a band around the reported depth; the dropdown says what share of real measurements fall inside it. The tool converts it to a standard deviation for the Normal sizing error: SD = tolerance / z, with z = 1 (1 SD), 1.2816 (80%), 1.6449 (90%), 1.96 (95%), two-sided.

Example: "depth ±10% WT at 80% certainty" on 0.322 in nominal gives ±32.2 mils and SD = 25.1 mils. Selecting "1 SD" instead would overstate the spread by about 28%; "95%" would understate it by about 35%. A larger SD fattens the early tail of TTF and raises early-year PoF, most strongly for deep features.

ILI-updated corrosion rate (ILI Update tab)

  • Why not just difference two ILI readings? Each reading carries sizing error. With 28% -> 48% WT over 5.68 yr on 0.322 in wall, the nominal rate is 11.4 mpy, but with +/-10% WT at 80% confidence on each run the rate is only known to about +/- 6.2 mpy (1 SD).
  • Absolute vs relative tolerance: vendor specs are normally "+/-10% of wall thickness". Reading it as "+/-10% of the reported depth" shrinks the band (30% -> 27-33% instead of 20-40%) and makes the measured rate look far more certain than it is (envelope 6.6-16.6 mpy instead of -1.3-24.5 mpy for the 30% -> 48%, 5-yr example).
  • Tolerance corners (min / nominal / max) show the extreme growth paths; they are not a confidence interval - both runs being at opposite extremes is unlikely. The 90% statistical interval is the better summary.
  • Prior, likelihood, posterior: the grey bars / area are fleet experience, the dashed curve is how well each rate explains this pipeline's readings, and the coloured result is their product. Rates outside the ILI envelope keep some weight unless the tolerance is set as a hard bound.
  • Conflict check: if the measured rate falls in the outer 2.5% of fleet experience, the fleet data may not represent the line (or the features are mismatched).
  • Non-detection: carry-forward is conservative; POD-weighting uses the fact that deep features are hard to miss (low-rate realizations keep their weight, high-rate ones lose it).
  • Back-test: about 80% of hidden readings should fall in the 80% interval; a positive bias (actual deeper than predicted) means the model is non-conservative.

Next ILI projection

  • Recommended next ILI = the earlier of (Last ILI + fixed interval for the process fluid) and (Last ILI + half the remaining life). The earlier date always rules.
  • Remaining life is counted from the Last ILI date because the remaining wall comes from that inspection. The default basis, Worst-Case P10 TTF, is the time by which 10% of simulated realizations have penetrated for the governing mechanism; P50 is the median; the deterministic option uses RW divided by the fastest observed rate.
  • Half remaining life follows the common integrity practice of re-inspecting before half the estimated remaining life is consumed, leaving margin for growth-rate and sizing uncertainty.
  • Status compares the recommended date with the assessment date: OVERDUE (already passed), Due within the window, or Not yet due. A planned date later than the recommended date is flagged, with the Worst-Case PoF reached by that date.
  • Fixed intervals are your inputs - set them from company standards and any applicable regulation; the sample values are placeholders.
Reading the other results
  • Cumulative PoF(t) - probability that through-wall penetration has occurred by the end of year t (primary metric). Interval PoF - probability it occurs during that year only; interval values are not cumulative.
  • Worst-Case PoF = MAX(Internal, External): the governing single mechanism. Combined PoF = probability that either mechanism leaks; always at least the Worst-Case value, never the sum.
  • 0.00% PoF means no realization penetrated in that period; the resolution is 1/N (0.001% at N = 100,000), not proof of zero probability.
  • Exact from data (Detailed calculation, bootstrap with ILI off) is the closed-form PoF from the observations; the Monte Carlo value converges to it as N grows.
  • PoF in this application means probability of modeled through-wall corrosion penetration / pinhole leakage - not burst, rupture, or total failure probability from all threats.

Model & Method

How the model works - ILI-updated (pipeline-specific) model

Left: the calculation chain, run separately for internal (IC) and external (EC) corrosion. Right: what each step does. Pipelines without ILI history skip steps 3-7 and use the fleet distribution directly (flow below).

Model flow - one pipeline (fleet distribution)

Each mechanism is simulated on its own random stream. Worst-Case (black) is the larger of the two mechanism PoFs at each year. Combined (purple, dashed = optional) is the probability that either mechanism leaks first; it is a different metric and is never the sum of the two.

Wall geometry - why the mechanisms are separate

Internal metal loss grows from the bore outward; external metal loss grows from the outer surface inward, usually at different locations. Each has its own remaining wall (RW) and its own corrosion rate, so each gets its own TTF and PoF.

Calculation chain

1 - Remaining wall
RW = Nominal WT x (1 - ML%/100)
RW_mils = RW_in x 1000
Direct remaining WT is used when given; ML% only fills gaps and is cross-checked.
2 - Sample corrosion rate
CR_i = random observed value
(or draw from fitted dist.)
Bootstrap never generates rates outside the uploaded observations.
3 - Time to through-wall
TTF_i = RW_mils / CR_i [yr]
CR_i = 0 -> TTF_i = infinity
Rate held constant over the remaining life in each realization.
4 - Mechanism PoF
PoF(t) = Count(TTF_i <= t) / N
Cumulative probability of penetration by the end of year t.
5 - Worst-case PoF
MAX(PoF_int(t), PoF_ext(t))
Controlling mechanism = the larger one; it can change with time.
6 - Combined PoF (optional)
Paired: Count(MIN(TTF_int,TTF_ext) <= t)/N
Indep.: 1 - (1-PoF_int)(1-PoF_ext)
Probability that either mechanism causes the first leak.
7 - Interval PoF
PoF(t) - PoF(t-1)
= P(t-1 < TTF <= t)
Probability of penetration during that year only (not cumulative).
Model Assumptions
  1. Failure is defined as localized through-wall penetration / pinhole leak.
  2. The model does not calculate burst or rupture probability. B31G, Modified B31G, RSTRENG and pressure capacity are not used.
  3. Internal and external corrosion are treated as separate failure mechanisms.
  4. Internal and external corrosion rates are not added together.
  5. In the base model, each Monte Carlo realization samples a corrosion rate and assumes that rate remains constant over the simulated remaining life.
  6. Variability between simulations represents uncertainty/variability in future corrosion behavior.
  7. Empirical bootstrap sampling uses the uploaded observed corrosion-rate values (with replacement, equal weight).
  8. The uploaded corrosion-rate datasets must be representative of the pipelines being evaluated.
  9. ILI sizing uncertainty is excluded unless the user enables it.
  10. Worst-Case PoF is defined as MAX(Internal PoF, External PoF).
  11. Combined PoF represents the probability that either mechanism causes a leak and is not the same as Worst-Case PoF.
  12. The calculated PoF is a model-based probability conditional on the uploaded data and assumptions.
  13. The calculated PoF is not an observed historical leak frequency.
  14. ILI-updated model: the fleet corrosion-rate distribution is the prior; each pipeline's ILI readings are evidence. P(CR | ILI) is proportional to P(ILI | CR) x P(CR). The likelihood assumes linear growth between inspections with the stated sizing error.
  15. Tolerance interpretation is an input: % of wall thickness (ILI convention) or % of reported depth; Normal with a stated confidence (z) or a hard bound (uniform). It is never assumed to be 1 SD. Errors of different runs are independent unless a correlation rho is entered.
  16. Rate stationarity: in scenario A each realization keeps one rate. Scenario B lets a user-specified share of realizations switch to max(current, fleet-drawn) rate; no acceleration probability is assumed.
  17. Fleet representativeness: fleet data must represent the line. A measured rate outside fleet experience is flagged, not removed.
  18. Limited inspections: two readings give one noisy rate; the update shrinks it toward the fleet distribution in proportion to that noise. One reading gives no rate (fleet distribution used).
  19. IC / EC classification is taken from the ILI. A call that switches between internal and external is flagged as a possible classification change. Unknown/ambiguous calls are not modelled in this version - classify them first.
  20. Non-detection of a previously reported feature is handled by the selected rule (carry forward, POD-weighted, or resolved with evidence). A real metal-loss feature cannot shrink.
  21. Survival: realizations that would already have leaked before the latest ILI can be excluded, because the line is known to be in service.
  22. Pressure integrity (optional) uses Modified B31G with constant defect length and is reported separately from leak PoF.
  23. Monte Carlo sampling uncertainty is about 1/sqrt(N); differences smaller than about 2 standard errors are not meaningful.
  24. The model gives a probabilistic engineering risk estimate; it does not predict the date on which a pipeline will fail.
Implementation notes
  • Paired Monte Carlo draws the internal and external rates in each realization from their own datasets on independent random streams. Because the uploaded CR datasets carry no joint (same-location/same-time) pairing, paired results converge to the independence formula; differences between the two methods are Monte Carlo noise. The paired structure is retained so correlated sampling can be added if paired data become available.
  • A CR of 0 gives TTF = infinity (no penetration in that realization).
  • Years are binned with a 1e-9 yr tolerance so floating-point division (e.g. 100/10) lands on the intended year: penetration counts toward year t when TTF ≤ t.
  • Percentiles use linear interpolation between order statistics (same as Excel PERCENTILE.INC). Standard deviation is the sample (n-1) value.
  • PoF resolution is 1/N (e.g. 0.001% at N = 100,000). A PoF of 0 means no realization penetrated within the period, not that the probability is zero.
  • Fitted Normal is truncated at 0 mpy by resampling. Lognormal is fitted to ln(CR) of positive values only (zeros excluded and flagged). Gamma uses method-of-moments.
  • With ILI uncertainty on, starting remaining wall = reported RW + sampled sizing error, resampled until 0 < RW ≤ nominal WT (up to 200 tries, then the reported value is used).
  • Each pipeline/mechanism uses its own random stream derived from (seed, Pipeline_ID, mechanism), so a pipeline's result does not change when the fleet is reordered.
How This Calculation Works - plain-language notes

For each pipeline, each corrosion mechanism is handled on its own. The remaining wall is converted to mils, divided by a corrosion rate drawn from the observed data, and the result is a time to through-wall penetration (TTF). Monte Carlo repeats this thousands of times using corrosion rates sampled from the observed uploaded data; the fraction of realizations that penetrate by year t is the PoF.

TTF percentiles: P10 TTF means 10% of simulated penetration times occur at or before that time; P50 is the median simulated penetration time; P90 means 90% occur at or before that time. Low TTF percentiles therefore describe the early (fast-corrosion) tail.
